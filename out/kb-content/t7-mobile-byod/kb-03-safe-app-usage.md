# Safe App Usage for Freight Workers

The apps on your phone are the front door to your freight accounts. A compromised load board app — or a fake one that looks like the real thing — can hand an attacker access to active loads, carrier contacts, and payment instructions. This is where freight account compromises begin.

## Install Only From Official App Stores

The real DAT Mobile, Truckstop GoApp, Samsara, and KeepTruckin apps are published in the Apple App Store and Google Play Store. That's where you get them. Full stop.

If someone sends you a link to download a freight app — via text, email, WhatsApp, or a QR code — do not tap it. Legitimate apps don't require you to bypass the app store to install them. An APK file (Android install package) sent via text is a red flag regardless of who appears to be sending it.

## Verify the Developer Before You Install

App store listings show the developer name. Check it before installing anything freight-related:

- DAT Mobile is published by **DAT Solutions**
- Truckstop GoApp is published by **Truckstop.com**
- Samsara is published by **Samsara Inc.**

Fake apps use names designed to look close: "DAT Freight Tools LLC," "TruckStop Mobile Ltd," or generic names like "Freight Load Manager." Check the install count as well — established freight apps used by tens of thousands of carriers have correspondingly large install numbers. A newly published app with 50 downloads is not the industry standard tool it claims to be.

## Question Excessive Permissions

Every app asks for permission to access parts of your device. A load board app needs location access and possibly camera access for document capture. It does not need access to your contacts, SMS messages, or microphone.

When an app requests permissions that don't match its stated function, that's a signal. Tap through to see exactly what each app has requested access to. On both iOS and Android, you can review and revoke permissions in your device settings without uninstalling the app.

## Sideloading Is Off — Keep It Off

On Android, a setting called "Install Unknown Apps" (or "Allow from this source") controls whether apps can be installed from outside the Play Store. This must remain disabled.

If someone — a "dispatcher," a "shipper contact," or anyone else — tells you to turn that setting on so you can install a freight app, refuse. Report it. No legitimate freight software requires you to disable a security setting to install it.

## QR Codes Are Not Safe by Default

A QR code is just a URL you can't read before you scan it. QR codes posted at truck stops, printed on BOLs from unfamiliar parties, or sent via text message can point to credential-harvesting pages or malicious app downloads. When your phone's camera shows the URL before you open it, read it. If you don't recognize the domain, don't proceed.

## Keep Apps Updated

App updates ship security fixes. Running an outdated version of a freight app means running with known vulnerabilities that the developer has already patched. Enable automatic updates for freight apps, or check for updates weekly.

## One Password Per Account

CargoNet's Q1 2025 data shows that freight account compromises are increasingly driven by credential theft, not just identity spoofing. An attacker who gets your DAT password will try that same password on your TMS, your email, and your fuel card account within minutes. This is called credential stuffing.

Your DAT password should not work anywhere else. Use a password manager to generate and store unique passwords for each freight platform. Enable MFA on every account that supports it.

---

The apps on your phone are freight industry tools — treat them with the same care you'd apply to a company computer. The attacker who gets into your load board account doesn't need your truck. They can redirect your loads, change your payment routing, and disappear before you reach the next weigh station.
