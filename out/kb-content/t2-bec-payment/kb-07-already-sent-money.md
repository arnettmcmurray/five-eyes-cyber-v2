# If You Already Sent Money: The First 60 Minutes

If you discover that a payment was sent to a fraudulent account — or that a payment request you approved may have been a BEC attack — the speed of your response determines how much is recoverable. Act within the first hour. Here is what to do.

## Minute 0–5: Stop Everything and Report Internally

Stop any additional payments that are pending. If multiple payments are in a queue tied to the same request or the same payee, hold them all immediately.

Tell your supervisor and company owner right now. Use your voice — call them, walk over, do not rely on email for this notification. Give them:
- The amount
- Where it was sent (bank name, account number, routing number if you have it)
- When it was sent
- The fraudulent communication you received

## Minute 5–20: Call Your Bank's Fraud Line

Call your bank's fraud department — not the general customer service number. Use the direct number from your bank statement, the back of your debit/credit card, or the bank's official website. Do not use a number from the payment confirmation email.

Say: "I need to initiate a wire recall / ACH reversal. The payment was fraudulent."

Provide: the transfer amount, destination account number and routing number, the exact date and time, and the reference number if you have it.

**This is the most time-sensitive step.** Domestic wire recalls within the first few hours have a meaningful recovery rate. International wires move faster and recovery becomes difficult within 24 hours.

## Minute 20–60: Document and Preserve Evidence

Do not delete anything. Preserve:
- The original fraudulent email (do not just save the body — export it with headers intact)
- Any attachments from the fraudulent communication
- Your payment records showing the amount, destination, and time
- Any approval records or authorization trail

Your bank, insurance carrier, and law enforcement will need this documentation.

## Hour 1–4: Notify Your Cyber Insurance Carrier

If you have a cyber liability, crime, or business insurance policy with cyber coverage, call the claims line now. Many policies require timely reporting (within 24 hours for some carriers) and have incident response resources that can help. Have your policy number and a summary of what happened ready.

## Hour 4–24: File with the FBI's IC3

File a complaint at IC3.gov. The FBI's Financial Fraud Kill Chain program works with financial institutions to halt and recover BEC funds. The sooner the complaint is filed, the more recovery options exist.

## What Not to Do

- **Do not send more money** to the fraudulent account or to recover the original payment — no legitimate recovery process works this way.
- **Do not contact the fraudulent account** directly — this may accelerate fund movement.
- **Do not delete evidence** — the email, the payment record, the correspondence.
- **Do not wait** to see if the bank sorts it out — they cannot act until you call them.

## The Hard Truth

Money that moves in a wire transfer is difficult to recover once it leaves the financial system — especially after 48–72 hours. The probability of recovery is highest in the first hour. This is not a situation where waiting to gather more information before acting is the right call. Act, then gather information.
