# Evidence Capture Checklist: What to Preserve When Fraud Is Suspected

When a payment fraud attempt is discovered, the quality and completeness of evidence you preserve determines the outcome of bank recovery attempts, insurance claims, and law enforcement investigations. This checklist applies as soon as fraud is suspected — do not wait for confirmation.

## Email Evidence

- [ ] Export the suspicious email with full headers (not just the message body). In most email clients: right-click the email → "View Message Source" or "Show Original." Save as .eml or .txt file.
- [ ] Note the sender's actual email address (not just the display name).
- [ ] Note the Reply-To address if different from the sender.
- [ ] Capture a screenshot of the email as it appears in your inbox, showing the sender field.
- [ ] Save any attachments from the fraudulent email without opening them on a network-connected device.

## Payment Records

- [ ] The payment confirmation number or reference number from your bank or payment system.
- [ ] The exact dollar amount.
- [ ] The destination bank name, routing number, and account number.
- [ ] The date and time the payment was initiated.
- [ ] The date and time the payment was confirmed as sent.
- [ ] The name on the destination account if visible.

## Authorization Trail

- [ ] Who requested the payment (the fraudulent email or communication).
- [ ] Who approved the payment in your system.
- [ ] Any verbal or written instructions that accompanied the request.
- [ ] Whether dual approval was completed, and if so, both approvers' names and the time of approval.

## Communication History

- [ ] All email correspondence from the fraudulent sender (not just the most recent email).
- [ ] Any phone call records: the number called or received from, approximate time, and what was discussed.
- [ ] Any chat or messaging records related to the payment request.

## Timeline Document

Create a simple chronological log:
- When the fraudulent communication was first received
- When it was acted on (if applicable)
- When fraud was suspected
- When fraud was confirmed
- Each action taken after discovery (who was called, when, what was said)

This timeline helps banks, insurers, and law enforcement reconstruct the incident quickly and supports your claim.

## If Your Email Was Compromised

If the attack involved a compromised email account (yours or a vendor's):
- [ ] Export and preserve the inbox and sent folder of the affected account before any changes are made.
- [ ] Note the date and time of suspicious sent messages.
- [ ] Identify any email forwarding rules, filters, or automatic actions that may have been set up by the attacker.
- [ ] Document login records or access logs if available from your email provider.

## Chain of Custody Note

Do not alter the original evidence — do not delete, edit, or move files. Preserve originals in a location that is not shared with the attacker's potential access. If a company email account was compromised, work with your IT or MSP to extract evidence without the attacker being able to delete it.

Once evidence is captured and preserved, share it with: your bank's fraud team, your cyber insurance carrier, and law enforcement. Keep a separate copy for your own records.
