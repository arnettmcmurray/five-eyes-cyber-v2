# Phishing in Freight: Recognizing Scam Emails Targeting Logistics Teams

Phishing emails are the most common entry point for cyberattacks against freight and logistics companies. Unlike the generic "Nigerian prince" scams, freight-targeted phishing is designed to look exactly like the emails your team already receives every day — load board alerts, delivery exception notices, FMCSA compliance reminders, and carrier setup requests. The deception works because it matches the urgency and format of legitimate freight communications.

## Why Freight Companies Are High-Value Targets

Freight operations move fast. Dispatchers process dozens of load tenders daily. Drivers get texts about delivery windows. Finance teams receive constant invoice and payment requests. Attackers know that when a dispatcher sees "Urgent: Load Board Match — Confirm Now," they are primed to click. That urgency is a feature, not a bug, for the attacker.

The FBI has documented how cyber cargo theft works: attackers use phishing emails to steal credentials, then access TMS portals, load boards, or email accounts to create fraudulent shipping paperwork, redirect loads, or initiate payment fraud. The phishing email is not the attack itself — it is the door.

## Common Freight Phishing Lures

**Load board notifications** — Fake emails from "[DAT / Truckstop / 123Loadboard]" claiming a high-value load match or account alert. The link goes to a credential-harvesting page that looks like the real portal.

**FMCSA or DOT compliance notices** — Emails claiming your authority is suspended, your MC number has a violation, or your DOT registration requires immediate action. Official FMCSA communications come through established channels — not unsolicited emails demanding urgent login.

**Carrier setup or onboarding requests** — "New carrier packet" emails with attachments containing malware, or links to fake carrier portal logins.

**Delivery exception texts and emails** — Fake package tracking alerts designed for warehouse and dock staff, often leading to malware downloads or credential forms.

**Driver portal password resets** — "Your ELD or TMS account needs verification" messages directing drivers to a spoofed login page.

**HR and payroll bait** — "Update your direct deposit information" emails sent to drivers or back-office staff, timed around payroll cycles.

**Invoice and payment requests** — Emails from spoofed vendor addresses requesting invoice payment to a new account. These overlap with BEC (Business Email Compromise) tactics.

## What Makes Freight Phishing Hard to Spot

- Attackers research your vendors. They may know which load board you use, which TMS you run, and who your factoring company is.
- The sender display name looks right ("DAT Support") even if the actual email address is wrong.
- The email may reference real load numbers, carrier names, or contact names scraped from public freight databases.
- Time pressure is built in — freight urgency is the social engineering.

## The Consequence Is Not Just a Compromised Account

A stolen dispatcher login to your TMS or load board account can enable a fraudster to accept loads on your behalf, divert shipments, or create fictitious pickup paperwork for cargo theft. A stolen finance login can expose every pending invoice and payment. A stolen email account can be used to send payment-redirect requests to your customers and vendors — and they will trust it because the email appears to come from you.

## What You Should Do

- **Do not click links in unsolicited emails** — go directly to the official site by typing the URL.
- **Verify urgency claims** — a real FMCSA notice will give you time; a phishing email creates false urgency.
- **Report suspicious emails** — see the reporting policy in this knowledge base.
- **Do not open unexpected attachments**, even from known contacts whose accounts may have been compromised.
- **If you clicked**, follow the "I clicked a link" response card immediately.

The goal is simple: make clicking the default response more expensive than pausing to verify. That pause is the training.
