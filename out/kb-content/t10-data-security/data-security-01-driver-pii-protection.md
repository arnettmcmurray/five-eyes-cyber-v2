# Driver PII in Freight: What You Collect, How to Protect It, and What Not to Do

Freight carriers and brokers collect some of the most sensitive categories of personal data that exist — Social Security numbers, medical records, drug test results, and precise location tracking. They often store it in shared folders with no access controls, transmit it over unencrypted email, and dispose of it by moving it to the recycle bin. That combination of high-value data and low-security handling is why driver records are a target.

This policy establishes what constitutes driver Personally Identifiable Information (PII) in freight, what federal law requires you to do with it, and what minimum controls apply.

## What Counts as Driver PII in Freight

Driver PII in freight operations includes, but is not limited to:

- **Full legal name and home address**
- **Social Security Number (SSN)** — collected for employment verification, tax purposes, and background checks
- **Commercial Driver's License (CDL) number and issuing state**
- **Date of birth (DOB)** — collected at hire and for CDL verification
- **Drug and alcohol test results** — pre-employment, random, post-accident, and return-to-duty results under DOT testing programs
- **Medical Examiner Certificate number** and the underlying medical examination results that determine DOT physical fitness status
- **Accident history** — DOT-reportable incidents and the company accident register
- **Employment verification records** — previous employer inquiries required by 49 CFR 391.23
- **GPS tracking and ELD location data** — real-time and historical location derived from Electronic Logging Device records, which are legally linked to an identified driver
- **Hours of service logs** — ELD records that document a named driver's location and activity over time

Each of these categories is independently sensitive. The combination — SSN, CDL, DOB, and accident history in a single driver qualification file — is a high-value target for identity fraud, synthetic identity creation, and false employment verification.

## FMCSA Retention Requirements

Federal regulation establishes minimum retention periods. These are legal floors, not suggestions.

- **Driver qualification files** (49 CFR 391.51): must be retained for the duration of employment plus three years after the employment relationship ends. Contents include the application, motor vehicle record, road test certificate, CDL copy, and previous employer inquiry results.
- **Accident register** (49 CFR 390.15): must be maintained for three years from the date of each accident.
- **Drug and alcohol testing records** (49 CFR Part 40 and 49 CFR 382.401): retention periods vary by record type — five years for positive test results and refusals; two years for negative pre-employment tests; one year for MIS annual reports. The employer is the custodian of record for all testing conducted under their DOT program.
- **Hazmat shipping papers**: 375 days under HMR 49 CFR 177.817.

## Minimum Protection Controls

1. **Secured storage location.** Driver qualification files must be stored in a locked physical cabinet or an access-controlled digital folder — not on a shared network drive accessible to all staff, not in a general email attachment folder, and not in a cloud storage location set to "anyone with the link."

2. **SSN transmission.** SSNs must never be transmitted via unencrypted email. If an SSN must be shared electronically, use a secure portal, encrypted email, or a password-protected document with the password delivered via a separate channel.

3. **Drug and alcohol test results.** Access is restricted to the HR manager and the DOT-designated employer representative. Supervisors do not have standing access. Results are not discussed in general team communications.

4. **ELD driving logs.** Retained per FMCSA hours-of-service requirements. Access restricted to safety and compliance staff — not available to all dispatch or administrative personnel.

5. **CDL numbers.** CDL numbers identify drivers to state licensing authorities and are useful for impersonation. Do not include CDL numbers in unsecured documents, general email correspondence, or load documentation visible to parties outside the employment relationship.

## Disposal Standards

Retention periods end. When they do, records must be destroyed — not archived, not moved to a general trash folder, not left in a decommissioned email inbox.

- **Paper records**: shred using a cross-cut or micro-cut shredder. Strip-cut shredding is not adequate for SSNs, CDL numbers, or medical records.
- **Digital records**: use a secure deletion tool that overwrites the storage location. Deleting a file and emptying the recycle bin does not remove the data from the storage medium.
- **Cloud-stored records**: verify that deletion in the application also removes the data from cloud storage and backup snapshots. Confirm with your cloud provider or IT contact.

## Breach Response for Driver PII

If SSN, CDL, DOB, drug test results, or medical records are exposed — whether by unauthorized access, email misdirection, or system compromise — affected drivers must be notified. The obligation is not discretionary.

Most U.S. states have data breach notification laws requiring written notification to affected individuals within 30 to 60 days of discovery. Several states have shorter windows: New York (SHIELD Act) and California (CCPA) require notification as quickly as is reasonably possible; New York additionally requires notification to the state Attorney General. Some state laws and sector-specific regulations require notification within 72 hours.

The FTC requires that companies handling PII implement "reasonable security measures." Reasonable security includes access controls, encrypted storage for sensitive categories of data, and staff trained in handling requirements. An exposed driver file in a shared Google Drive is not reasonable security.

When in doubt about notification obligations, consult legal counsel immediately. The clock on notification windows starts at discovery, not at the conclusion of the investigation.
