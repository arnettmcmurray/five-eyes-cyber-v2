# You Think a BOL or POD Was Altered or Intercepted — Do This Now

Something does not match: the POD signature is wrong, the BOL destination changed after tender, the delivery address was redirected mid-transit, or the weight on the POD does not match the BOL. You suspect document fraud. Every minute you spend processing the transaction normally is a minute the attacker has to complete the theft. Stop the transaction and follow these steps.

---

**1. Hold the payment or cargo release — do not proceed until the document is verified.**

Do not approve the invoice, release the payment, or allow the cargo to move based on a document you suspect has been altered. The entire point of document fraud is to make the transaction look normal enough to process. If something flagged your attention, trust it.

Verbal pressure from the carrier or a broker to approve quickly is itself a warning sign. Legitimate parties understand that document discrepancies require verification.

**2. Call the shipper, receiver, or carrier on a known-good number to confirm the document.**

Use the phone number you had on file before this situation started — not a number provided in the suspect email or document, not a number provided by the person asking you to release the payment or cargo.

Ask specifically:
- Did you authorize this delivery destination?
- Is this signature from your receiving staff?
- Did you send these updated delivery instructions?
- Is this the correct commodity weight?

If the answer to any of these is "no" or "I don't know," the document is compromised. Do not proceed.

**3. Preserve the suspect document — do not delete, modify, or "correct" it.**

Do not edit the document to fix the discrepancy. Do not delete the email it came in. Do not overwrite the file. The suspect document and everything around it — the email thread, the delivery record, the original BOL — is evidence. Print a copy if the original is electronic. Note the time you identified the issue.

If the document came via email, preserve the full email with headers (do not just print the visible message — forward it to IT with the instruction to preserve headers).

**4. Notify your dispatch supervisor and management immediately.**

This is not a clerical dispute to resolve at the operational level. A suspected altered BOL or POD is a potential cargo theft or payment fraud event. Management must know now so they can:
- Decide whether to engage legal counsel
- Notify cargo insurance if a load may be affected
- Determine whether law enforcement reporting is appropriate
- Place other recent documents from the same carrier or contact under review

**5. Report to CargoNet if cargo is or may be in danger.**

CargoNet (cargonet.com / 1-800-227-4632) is the cargo theft intelligence network. If a suspicious document is connected to a load that may have been stolen, redirected, or not delivered, report immediately. CargoNet alerts law enforcement and other carriers in real time. Timely reporting significantly increases recovery probability for high-value cargo.

If you have reason to believe cargo has already been physically stolen (the truck is not at the expected location, the driver cannot be reached, the consignee never received delivery), also notify state law enforcement and file with FBI IC3 at ic3.gov.

**6. If funds were already moved: call your bank immediately and file an FBI IC3 report.**

If a payment was released based on the fraudulent document, contact your bank's fraud line right now — before doing anything else on this list. The Financial Fraud Kill Chain operated by FinCEN has a 72-hour window for wire recall. Every hour reduces recovery probability. After calling the bank, file a report at ic3.gov. Include the recipient bank name, account number if known, amount, date and time of transfer, and all documentation related to the fraudulent payment instruction.

---

**If the document was fraudulent:** Your bank, your cargo insurer, CargoNet, and FBI IC3 are the four notification targets. Contact them in that order based on what was actually harmed — funds, cargo, or both.
