# TSA Security Directive 1580-21-01 Version C: What Covered Operators Must Do

TSA Security Directive 1580-21-01 is the primary federal cybersecurity mandate for freight railroad operators. Version C is currently in effect as of October 2024 and is subject to annual renewal. This is not a proposed rule — it is a binding requirement for designated covered entities.

## Who It Covers

The directive applies to **designated freight railroad operators and pipeline owners/operators** identified by TSA. Coverage is determined by TSA based on the national security significance of the operations.

**Trucking is not currently covered by this directive.** The expansion of TSA cyber requirements to surface transportation operators — including trucking — is the subject of a separate Notice of Proposed Rulemaking issued in November 2024. That NPRM is proposed, not final. Trucking operators should monitor that rulemaking for the final rule.

## Core Requirements

**1. Designate a Primary Cybersecurity Coordinator**

Covered operators must designate a primary cybersecurity coordinator and an alternate. Both individuals must be reachable 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and their current contact information must be on file with TSA. This is not a title — it is an operational requirement. The coordinator must be able to act and communicate during an incident at any hour.

**2. Report Significant Cybersecurity Incidents to CISA Within 24 Hours**

When a covered operator identifies a significant cybersecurity incident, that incident must be reported to CISA within 24 hours of the identification. This clock does not start when the investigation is complete — it starts when the incident is identified as significant. The report should include what is known at the time, with updates to follow as the investigation progresses.

What qualifies as "significant" under the directive:
- Any incident that disrupts, or has the potential to disrupt, operations
- Evidence of ongoing unauthorized access to operational systems
- Ransomware or a destructive cyberattack targeting operational technology or IT systems

When in doubt, report. The directive does not penalize good-faith over-reporting.

**3. Develop and Maintain a Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan**

Covered operators must have a written cybersecurity incident response plan that documents roles, responsibilities, escalation procedures, communication chains, and recovery steps. The plan must be maintained — meaning it is reviewed and updated as systems, personnel, and threats change.

**4. Conduct an Annual Cybersecurity Architecture Review**

Each year, covered operators must conduct a review of their cybersecurity architecture — the systems, network configurations, access controls, and monitoring capabilities that protect operational technology and IT. The review identifies gaps and changes since the previous year.

## Annual Renewal

Version C took effect in October 2024. The directive is reviewed and renewed annually by TSA. Covered entities should verify the current version directly with TSA each October. If the version has changed, review any updated requirements before the next renewal cycle.

## The November 2024 NPRM — What's Coming for Trucking

In November 2024, TSA issued a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking proposing to extend cyber risk management requirements to approximately 300 surface transportation operators, including trucking carriers and bus operators. The proposed requirements track the existing directive closely: coordinator designation, incident reporting, incident response plans, and annual reviews.

This is a **proposed rule, not a final requirement.** The rulemaking process includes public comment, review, and eventual publication of a final rule — which has not yet occurred as of early 2026. Trucking operators should:

- Monitor the Federal Register and TSA announcements for the final rule
- Begin building incident response plan capability now, so compliance does not require starting from scratch when the rule is finalized
- Identify who in the organization would serve as a cybersecurity coordinator under the proposed framework

The time between a proposed rule and its effective date is the right time to prepare, not to wait.
