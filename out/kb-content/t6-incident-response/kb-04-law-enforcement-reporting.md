# Reporting to Law Enforcement: FBI/IC3, FMCSA, and Why It Matters

Most freight operators who experience cyber-enabled fraud do not report it to law enforcement. They resolve it internally, absorb the loss, and move on. That decision protects no one — not the company that was hit, and not the next carrier on the same attacker's list.

## FBI/IC3 — Internet Crime Complaint Center

**Where:** ic3.gov

**Who:** Any company that experiences cyber-enabled crime — BEC wire fraud, ransomware, phishing that resulted in financial loss, cargo theft with a cyber component, or carrier identity theft.

**Why it matters even when recovery is unlikely:** Every IC3 report feeds sector intelligence. FBI analysts identify patterns across complaints — the same fraudulent bank account used against three different carriers, the same spoofed domain targeting brokers on DAT. Your report makes the next investigation faster.

**When recovery is actually possible — the Financial Fraud Kill Chain:**
For BEC and wire fraud, the FBI's Financial Fraud Kill Chain (FFKC) is a real mechanism for attempting to freeze or recall fraudulent wire transfers. But it only works if you report fast enough. The 72-hour window from the time of the fraudulent transfer is critical. After funds move offshore, the recovery window closes. File the IC3 report immediately — do not wait until the internal investigation is complete.

**What to include in your IC3 report:**
- The dollar amounts involved
- The sending and receiving account numbers and bank names
- A complete timeline from first contact to transfer
- Copies of the fraudulent emails, texts, or messages — do not delete originals
- Any usernames, email addresses, phone numbers, or domains used by the attacker
- A description of how the fraud was executed

## FMCSA — Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration

**When to contact FMCSA:** When someone has stolen your carrier identity — fraudulently using your USDOT number, MC authority, or company name to book loads, pick up freight, or pose as your company to shippers.

**What to do:** File a complaint with FMCSA and immediately notify the load boards — DAT and Truckstop — that your identity has been compromised. Ask both platforms to flag your USDOT and MC numbers and to alert brokers posting loads to verify before tendering.

FMCSA maintains carrier identity records and can assist in documenting the fraud and alerting the broader system.

## State Fusion Centers

For cargo theft incidents that involve a cyber component — fraudulent load tenders, fake pickup authorization, spoofed broker identity — state fusion centers aggregate intelligence from law enforcement agencies across state lines. Most cargo theft crosses multiple jurisdictions. Contact your state law enforcement agency and ask about fusion center reporting.

## Why Report When Nothing Will Be Recovered

Law enforcement priorities and federal task force funding follow the data. The FBI's freight fraud task forces, FMCSA enforcement resources, and TSA surface transportation security initiatives are all shaped by the volume and detail of incident reports. A carrier that absorbs a $50,000 BEC loss and files nothing has contributed to the statistical invisibility of freight fraud. A carrier that reports it has contributed to the case that gets the next task force funded and the next arrest made.

Report every time. Even when the money is gone.
