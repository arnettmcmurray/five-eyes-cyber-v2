# Load Board Red Flags: Patterns That Indicate Fraud

The FBI has explicitly warned freight industry participants to exercise increased caution when using online load boards and to verify driver and equipment identifiers at pickup. Load boards are the primary venue where cargo theft-by-deception begins. This brief catalogs the specific patterns that indicate a freight fraud attempt.

## Red Flag 1: Suspiciously Competitive Rates

A carrier offering a notably low rate for a load — especially a high-value, easy-to-liquidate commodity — is one of the oldest indicators of a fraudulent carrier. Legitimate carriers price to cover fuel, maintenance, insurance, and driver wages. Rates significantly below market for the lane and load type may indicate the carrier is not planning to complete the delivery legitimately.

Combine this with other red flags — new authority, unverifiable contacts — and it is a strong warning.

## Red Flag 2: Very New or Recently Restored Authority

Check the carrier's authority date in FMCSA. Carriers that obtained their MC number within the last 30–90 days accepting high-value loads is a documented fraud pattern. Similarly, a carrier whose authority was revoked and very recently restored should receive extra scrutiny.

Legitimate new carriers exist. The red flag is the combination: new authority + high-value load + competitive rate + urgency.

## Red Flag 3: Contact Information That Does Not Match FMCSA

When a carrier's submitted phone number or email does not match the information in their FMCSA record, this is a warning that the contact information has been modified — possibly by someone who hijacked the carrier profile or created a fraudulent one.

**How to check:** Look up the carrier in FMCSA and compare the contact information displayed there against what you received on the load board or in the carrier's direct outreach.

## Red Flag 4: Pressure to Accept Quickly

Urgency pressure — "you need to confirm in the next 30 minutes or I'm taking another load" — is used to prevent you from doing verification steps. Legitimate carriers understand that business processes include verification. Artificial urgency to skip steps is a warning sign.

## Red Flag 5: Equipment or Driver Changes After Confirmation

Once a carrier is confirmed and equipment is verified, any last-minute change to the driver name, tractor/trailer identifiers, or pickup timing — especially communicated by text or a new email thread rather than through your established contact — requires re-verification before proceeding.

Cargo theft operations sometimes arrange a legitimate-seeming initial verification and then substitute a fraudulent driver at the last moment.

## Red Flag 6: Double-Brokering Indicators

Double brokering is when a broker accepts a load and then brokers it to a second carrier without the shipper's knowledge or authorization. The first broker may disappear without paying the second carrier, or the shipper ends up without accountability for the load's handling.

Indicators of double brokering:
- The carrier who accepted the load turns out to have a different USDOT/MC number than what is on the dispatch record.
- The driver at pickup presents paperwork showing a carrier name different from the one you engaged with.
- The carrier calls to say they "passed the load" to another driver or company.
- Payment disputes that reveal multiple parties claim to have brokered the same load.

If the carrier who arrives does not match the carrier you confirmed, do not release the load. Contact your verified broker or dispatch contact directly before proceeding.

## Red Flag 7: Load Board Profile Recently Modified

Some load boards show when a profile was last updated. A profile that was significantly modified (contact info, equipment listed, rates) shortly before submitting a bid on your load deserves extra scrutiny.

## Red Flag 8: Payment or Communication via Personal Channels

A carrier or broker representative insisting on payment to a personal account (not the company account on file), or insisting on communications through a personal email or WhatsApp rather than the business contact you verified — this is a red flag for a diverted process.

## The Pattern Recognition Rule

No single red flag is definitive. But multiple red flags appearing together — new authority, competitive rate, urgency, mismatched contact info — are a strong composite indicator of fraud. When you see two or more red flags on the same load, stop and verify before proceeding.

Trust the pattern, not the paperwork.
