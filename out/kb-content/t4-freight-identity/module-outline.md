# Module: Freight Identity, Verification, and Fraud Controls

## Module Purpose
Teach dispatch, dock staff, brokerage operations, and management how cargo theft-by-deception works and what verification behaviors stop it. This is the most freight-specific module in the first tranche — it has no direct analog in generic office security training. Emphasis on practical scenario judgment at each workflow stage.

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this module, learners will be able to:
1. Identify the stages of the freight workflow where identity fraud and cargo theft occur.
2. Apply carrier identity verification using FMCSA and an independent callback.
3. Recognize red flags at each workflow stage (tender, vetting, pickup, in-transit).
4. Execute the pickup integrity checklist before releasing a load.
5. Handle high-risk exceptions (destination changes, carrier swaps) using the verification policy.
6. Explain how double brokering works and how to detect it.

## Module Structure

### Lesson 1: How Cargo Theft Actually Works Today (7 min)
**KB source:** kb-01-freight-fraud-kill-chain.md

**Core idea:** Modern cargo theft is not primarily physical crime — it is deception. The FBI documents identity theft, fictitious pickups, account takeovers, and double brokering as the dominant patterns. The load leaves your dock because someone convincingly impersonated a legitimate carrier or manipulated the verification process. The kill chain has six stages, and every stage has a verification point that can break it.

**Scenario:** Walk through a complete fictitious pickup operation from the attacker's perspective: fake USDOT profile on load board → accepts tender → provides fake carrier packet → arrives at dock with convincing paperwork → dock releases load. At which stage did the defense fail?

### Lesson 2: Carrier Identity Verification (7 min)
**KB source:** kb-02-carrier-identity-verification.md, kb-03-load-board-red-flags.md

**Core idea:** Load board profiles can be faked. Carrier packets can be forged. FMCSA is the authoritative source for carrier identity. The verification call — using a number you find yourself in FMCSA — is the control that the load board cannot substitute for.

**Scenario (dispatch):** "A carrier bids on your load at a competitive rate. Their load board profile looks fine. Walk through the verification steps before you confirm them."

**Scenario (brokerage):** "New carrier contacts you directly with a carrier packet. Everything looks professional. What do you verify and how?"

### Lesson 3: Red Flags in Practice (5 min)
**KB source:** kb-03-load-board-red-flags.md, kb-08-red-flags-by-workflow-stage.md

**Core idea:** Red flags cluster — no single flag is definitive, but two or more at the same stage means escalate before proceeding. The pattern recognition skill is built through scenario practice.

**Scenario exercise:** Present a load scenario with 3 embedded red flags. Learner must identify each one and state what action to take.

### Lesson 4: Pickup Integrity — The Last Control (5 min)
**KB source:** kb-05-pickup-integrity.md, kb-09-role-based-verification-checklists.md

**Core idea:** The pickup moment is the last opportunity to catch a fictitious pickup. Driver name, equipment identifiers, and pickup code must all match before the load is released. A mismatch is a hold, not a minor issue.

**Scenario (dock staff):** "A driver arrives for a load. Their name is different from the appointment. They say the driver was swapped last minute and show you a text from 'dispatch.' What do you do?"

**Role-specific focus:** Dock staff version focuses on the physical checklist. Dispatch version focuses on managing the exception call process.

### Lesson 5: Documents Are Not Proof (3 min)
**KB source:** kb-06-bol-pod-document-integrity.md, kb-07-exception-handling-rules.md

**Core idea:** A professional-looking BOL, carrier authority certificate, or insurance document is not evidence of a legitimate transaction — they can all be forged or modified. Document integrity comes from the verification process, not the document itself.

**The rule:** A document supports a verified transaction. It does not replace verification.

### Lesson 6: Double Brokering and What to Do When the Load Doesn't Add Up (3 min)
**KB source:** kb-04-double-brokering-mechanics.md

**Core idea:** Double brokering is when the carrier who accepted your load has re-brokered it to someone else without authorization. The driver at your dock may not be the carrier you contracted. If the paperwork shows a different carrier name or MC number — stop and call dispatch.
