# Carrier Identity Verification: What to Check and Where

Carrier identity fraud — where a fraudster impersonates a legitimate carrier or creates a fictitious one to steal a load — relies on victims not verifying the carrier's credentials independently. This article defines what must be verified, where to verify it, and how to handle discrepancies.

## Why Load Board Profiles Are Not Enough

A carrier's listing on a load board (DAT, Truckstop.com, etc.) shows information the carrier or someone using their credentials submitted. Load board profiles can be:
- Created using a hijacked USDOT/MC number.
- Modified after initial verification to show different equipment or contact details.
- Associated with email and phone contact information controlled by the fraudster.

Load board "verified" badges and ratings provide some signal but are not a substitute for independent verification against official government records.

## The Primary Verification Source: FMCSA

The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) maintains the authoritative database of carrier registrations, operating authority, and safety records. Use these for every new carrier and for any carrier not in your verified carrier file:

**Website:** [fmcsa.dot.gov](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov) → Carrier search
**Direct carrier lookup:** [li.fmcsa.dot.gov/hhg/lookup.asp](https://li.fmcsa.dot.gov/hhg/lookup.asp)

**What to check in FMCSA:**
- **Operating authority status:** Must be "Authorized for Property" (or the relevant authority type for the load). "Not Authorized" means they cannot legally haul your load.
- **Authority age:** Very recently issued authority (days or weeks old) combined with a competitive rate and high-value load is a risk pattern.
- **Insurance status:** The carrier's cargo insurance must be active. FMCSA shows the insurance on file.
- **Safety rating:** Check for safety ratings and inspection history. A carrier with "Unsatisfactory" safety rating is a concern beyond fraud risk.
- **Match contact info:** The phone number and address in FMCSA should generally align with what the carrier gave you. Significant mismatches are a red flag.

## The Callback Step

After FMCSA verification, call the carrier at the phone number shown in FMCSA records — not the number in the load board listing or the carrier's submitted contact. If FMCSA shows a different number than what was submitted to you, call both and note the discrepancy.

The purpose of this call is to confirm:
- They are aware of the load you are discussing.
- The driver assigned to the load is their driver.
- The equipment (tractor plate, trailer plate) matches what is in your records.

If the number in FMCSA does not reach anyone, or reaches someone who has no knowledge of the load — stop. Do not proceed until you have spoken with a verified contact at the carrier.

## The Carrier Packet Problem

Carrier packets (onboarding documents sent by email) often contain:
- Insurance certificates
- W-9 for payment
- Bank details
- Contact information

The information in a carrier packet may all be fraudulent. A professional-looking PDF does not verify the bank details in it. A forged insurance certificate looks like a real one.

**The rule:** Carrier packet documents confirm what the carrier claims about themselves. Independent verification through FMCSA and a callback confirms what is actually true.

## Equipment Identifier Verification at Pickup

At the pickup appointment, the driver must present and the dock must confirm:
- **Driver's CDL or ID** — matches the name on the load appointment.
- **Tractor plate number** — matches what dispatch confirmed with the carrier.
- **Trailer plate number** — matches.
- **Seal number** (for sealed trailers) — confirm before release and record on BOL.
- **Pickup code** — the driver must know the code communicated to the carrier advance of pickup, via a channel separate from the one used to arrange the pickup.

A driver who cannot produce the pickup code should not receive the load, regardless of what other paperwork they have.

## Red Flags That Require Escalation

- Carrier's FMCSA authority is very new (days or weeks old).
- Operating authority is suspended or inactive.
- Phone number in the carrier packet does not match FMCSA records.
- The FMCSA number does not reach anyone with knowledge of the load.
- Driver's equipment identifiers do not match what dispatch arranged.
- Driver does not know the pickup code.
- Any last-minute change to the driver name, equipment, or pickup instructions — especially if communicated by email or text rather than through your established dispatch contact.

Escalate any of these to your supervisor before releasing the load. A delayed pickup is recoverable. A stolen load is not.
