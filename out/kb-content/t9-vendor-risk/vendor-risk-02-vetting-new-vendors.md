# Vendor Vetting Minimum Standards for Freight Operations

No vendor is granted access to business systems, sensitive data, or financial processes without completing the vetting requirements in this policy. This applies to technology vendors, managed service providers, staffing firms with system access, freight technology platforms, and any contractor who will be given login credentials to company systems.

This is not a courtesy checklist. It is a minimum security requirement. NIST SP 800-161 Rev.1 (C-SCRM: Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management) identifies vendor due diligence as a core function of supply chain risk management — not optional for any organization handling sensitive data or critical operations. A freight operation's TMS, ELD portal, factoring platform, and payroll system all qualify.

Think of this the same way you think about carrier vetting. You do not put freight with an unverified carrier — you check their authority, insurance, and safety rating before you tender a load. The same discipline applies to technology partners. You are trusting them with access that, if misused or compromised, could expose every customer, every carrier relationship, and every dollar that flows through your operation.

## Pre-Access Requirements

Before any vendor is granted access to business systems, the following must be completed and documented:

**1. Identity Verification**

Confirm the vendor is who they say they are before the relationship begins. Verify the business is legitimately registered (state business registry lookup takes five minutes). For carrier or broker technology vendors, check FMCSA to confirm their operating authority is active. Call the main company phone number — found independently, not provided by the sales contact — and confirm that the person you are working with is actually employed there. Impersonation of legitimate vendors is a documented fraud tactic; do not skip this step because the vendor "seems legitimate."

**2. Scope Definition**

Document in writing exactly what access the vendor needs and why before provisioning anything. Access should be the minimum required to perform the contracted function — not "admin access to everything because it is easier." A vendor brought in to implement a new TMS module does not need access to your payroll system. A fuel card account manager does not need admin access to your email. Scope creep in vendor access is how overprivileged vendor accounts happen; prevent it before provisioning, not after.

**3. Security Attestation for Cloud and SaaS Vendors**

Any vendor that will access, store, or transmit your business data must provide evidence of a security program. The acceptable minimum is:

- SOC 2 Type II audit report (within the past 12 months), or
- ISO 27001 certification (current), or
- If neither is available: a completed written security questionnaire signed by the vendor's named CISO, IT director, or security lead — not a generic sales form

For very small vendors without formal certifications, the security questionnaire is not optional. Ask specifically about: access controls for their employees who will touch your data, multi-factor authentication requirements, incident response and notification procedures, and data retention and destruction practices.

**4. Background Screening for Vendors with Admin-Level Access**

Vendors who will hold administrator-level access to your systems — MSPs, IT consultants, cloud platform administrators — must confirm that the staff members assigned to your account have undergone background screening. This requirement belongs in the vendor agreement. If the vendor cannot confirm staff background screening, admin-level access is not appropriate.

**5. Reference Check**

Ask the vendor specifically about security incidents in the past two years — not general references about service quality. Did they experience a breach? How was it handled? Were clients notified promptly? A vendor's answer to this question is more informative than their marketing materials.

**6. Written Agreement with Required Security Clauses**

No vendor accesses your systems without a signed agreement. At minimum, the agreement must include:

- Notification within 24 hours of any security incident or breach affecting your data or systems
- Right to audit vendor access logs on reasonable notice
- Immediate compliance with access revocation upon contract termination or security incident
- Data return or certified destruction upon offboarding
- Prohibition on sharing your credentials or data with subcontractors without prior written consent

A vendor that refuses to sign an agreement containing these terms is telling you something important about how they handle security. That is relevant information.

## Policy Ownership

The owner of each vendor relationship is responsible for ensuring these requirements are met before access is granted and for maintaining the vendor access register entry. Compliance with this policy is not delegated to the vendor.
