# Lost Phone: MFA Recovery Step-by-Step

If your phone is lost or stolen and you use it for MFA (authenticator app or SMS codes), you need to act quickly — both to regain access to your accounts and to prevent an attacker from using your phone to approve unauthorized logins.

## Step 1: Report the Loss Immediately

Tell your supervisor and IT/MSP contact. They can help coordinate account access recovery and may be able to remotely wipe a company-issued device.

If the phone is company-issued: contact your IT/MSP right away. Do not wait.

## Step 2: Secure Your Most Critical Accounts Using Recovery Codes

If you stored your MFA recovery codes (as recommended), use them now to access your accounts and update the MFA method. Start with:

1. Business email — this is your recovery path for everything else.
2. TMS account.
3. Load board accounts.
4. Payment portals.

**To use a recovery code:** On the login screen, when the MFA prompt appears, look for a link that says "Use a recovery code" or "Having trouble?" Click it and enter one of your backup codes.

After regaining access, immediately change the MFA setting to remove the lost phone and add your replacement device.

## Step 3: If You Did Not Save Recovery Codes

Contact each service's support directly. You will typically need to:
- Verify your identity (usually your email, phone number on account, or ID verification)
- Explain that you lost your MFA device
- Request account recovery

This process can take hours to days depending on the service. For critical business accounts, this delay can be operationally significant — which is why saving recovery codes is important.

## Step 4: Remove the Lost Phone From Your MFA Setup

Once you have access to each account, go to the security settings and:
- Remove the lost phone as an MFA device.
- Add your new phone.
- Generate new recovery codes (old ones may now be invalid after recovery).

## Step 5: Check for Unauthorized Access During the Window

After recovering access, review each account for activity during the period the phone was missing:
- Review recent logins and active sessions (look for unfamiliar locations or devices).
- Check email for sent messages you did not write.
- Check for forwarding rules or filter changes in email.
- Check payment accounts for unauthorized activity.

If you find evidence of unauthorized access, treat this as a full account compromise and follow the compromised account response procedure.

## Step 6: Prevent This From Happening Again

After recovering access:
- Save your recovery codes in your password manager, not only on the phone.
- Consider setting up a backup MFA method (a second device, or a hardware key) for critical accounts.
- Make sure your IT/MSP knows where your recovery documentation is stored.

## The Short Version

Lost phone → Tell supervisor and IT → Use recovery codes to access email first → Remove old phone from MFA → Add new phone → Check for unauthorized activity.
