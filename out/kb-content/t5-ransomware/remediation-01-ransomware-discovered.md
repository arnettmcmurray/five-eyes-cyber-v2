# Ransomware Discovered — Act Now

You have seen a ransom note, found files you cannot open with unfamiliar extensions, or been told by IT that ransomware is active on the network. Every minute matters. Follow these steps in order.

---

**1. Do not turn off the computer.**
Shutting down destroys forensic evidence that IT and investigators need. Leave powered systems on unless IT explicitly tells you to shut down.

**2. Stop working on the affected device.**
Do not save files, open applications, or browse the network. Additional activity can spread the infection or overwrite evidence.

**3. Write down what you see — on paper, right now.**
- What device is affected (workstation name, location)
- What you observed (ransom note, files with new extensions, inaccessible drives)
- The exact time you first noticed it
- Any unusual activity in the hours before (slow system, failed logins, antivirus alerts)

**4. Disconnect from the network — but only if IT instructs you to.**
If IT tells you to unplug ethernet and disable Wi-Fi, do it immediately. If you cannot reach IT and you are watching active encryption in progress, disconnect as a last resort and continue trying to reach IT.

**5. Call IT or your security contact now — by phone, not email.**
Do not send email from the compromised system. Use your phone. If you do not have the number memorized, it should be posted near your workstation. Report what you wrote down in Step 3.

**6. Notify operations leadership.**
Your manager or operations leadership needs to know immediately so they can activate offline procedures and escalate up the chain. This is not optional.

**7. Activate offline operations.**
While IT manages containment: switch to paper BOLs, phone-based driver check-in, and manual HOS logs. Do not wait for system restoration before resuming freight movement.

**8. Do not attempt to pay the ransom yourself.**
Payment decisions require executive leadership, legal counsel, and cyber insurance involvement. No individual employee is authorized to make or facilitate a ransom payment. Escalate; do not act.

**9. Do not tell customers or partners it is ransomware until leadership approves that message.**
Acknowledge a system issue is being addressed. Provide manual load status if asked. Wait for authorized communications guidance before disclosing the nature of the incident.
