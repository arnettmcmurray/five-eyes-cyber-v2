# ELD/TMS Systems Offline — Operational Continuity Steps

For dispatchers and fleet managers. Your electronic systems are down. Freight still moves. Here is how.

---

## For Dispatchers

**1. Switch to phone-based driver check-in immediately.**
Establish a scheduled check-in cadence — every 2 hours minimum for active loads. Record driver name, location, load status, and ETA on paper. Assign one dispatcher per 10–15 drivers to avoid phone bottleneck.

**2. Use paper BOLs for all new loads.**
Pre-printed blank BOL forms must be physically present at every terminal. Each BOL requires: shipper name and address, consignee name and address, commodity description, piece count, weight, any special handling requirements, and driver signature. Contact the shipper directly to confirm these details if not already documented.

**3. Document every assignment in writing.**
Your paper dispatch log is the record that billing will reconstruct from when TMS is restored. Write down: driver, load number or shipper reference, origin, destination, pickup time, delivery window, and rate if known. Date and time every entry.

**4. Notify shippers with active loads.**
Call each shipper with freight in transit. Tell them you are experiencing a system issue, give them a direct phone number for status updates, and provide a realistic ETA based on your last phone check-in with the driver. Do not promise TMS-based updates you cannot currently deliver.

**5. Suspend new load booking until you have IT guidance on system status.**
Taking on new loads without TMS access while managing active loads creates documentation gaps. Coordinate with operations leadership on whether to continue accepting new freight during the outage.

---

## For Fleet Managers

**6. Authorize paper HOS logs for all affected drivers immediately.**
Under FMCSA regulations, drivers may use paper logs when an ELD malfunctions. Send a written or verbal authorization to all drivers whose ELD is affected. Confirm drivers have paper log books in their cab — if not, direct them to the nearest terminal to obtain one.

**7. Instruct drivers on paper log completion.**
Drivers who haven't used paper logs recently may need a reminder: record duty status changes in real time (not retroactively), include total miles driven, carrier name, home terminal, and the reason for paper log use ("ELD malfunction — [date]"). Retain paper logs for 8 days.

**8. Apply for FMCSA ELD exemption if the outage extends beyond 8 days.**
An 8-day paper log period is the regulatory tolerance for ELD malfunction. If the system outage will exceed 8 days, contact FMCSA to request an emergency exemption. Document the outage start date, cause (system attack/outage), and affected drivers. The ORBCOMM incident precedent shows FMCSA has issued temporary exemptions during extended technology outages — but the carrier must request it.

**9. Maintain driver contact log centrally.**
In the absence of ELD location data, all driver contact and location information should flow to a single coordinator who maintains a master status board — physical whiteboard or paper log. Update it with every driver check-in.

**10. Preserve all paper documentation.**
Every paper log, BOL, and dispatch note from the outage period is a legal and billing record. Do not discard any of it. Designate a physical location for collection and organize by date.
