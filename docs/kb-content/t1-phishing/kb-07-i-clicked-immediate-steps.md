# I Clicked a Suspicious Link — What Do I Do Right Now?

If you clicked a link in an email or text and you are not sure whether it was legitimate, do not wait and hope for the best. Fast action limits the damage. Here is exactly what to do.

## Step 1 — Do Not Click Anything Else on the Page

If a page opened, do not enter any information. Do not enter your username, password, or any personal details. If the page downloaded a file, do not open it.

## Step 2 — Disconnect the Device From the Network

If you believe malware may have been downloaded:
- **On a company computer:** Disconnect from Wi-Fi and unplug any ethernet cable immediately.
- **On your phone:** Turn on airplane mode.
- **Do not turn off the device** unless instructed by IT — powered-on devices preserve logs that help with investigation.

If the link only opened a webpage and you are confident nothing was downloaded, you do not need to disconnect — but proceed to the next steps.

## Step 3 — Report Immediately

Tell your supervisor or security contact right now. Do not wait to see if anything happens. Key information to provide:
- The email or text where you found the link.
- Whether you entered any information on the page that opened.
- The approximate time you clicked.
- The device you used.

There is no penalty for reporting. Reporting enables the company to respond before a small incident becomes a large one.

## Step 4 — Change Your Password for Any Account That Opened in a Browser

If the link opened a page that looked like a login page — even if you did not enter your credentials — change the password for the service it was impersonating. It is possible the page attempted to capture a session cookie or other information without you entering anything.

If you did enter a password, change it immediately on every service that uses the same or similar password.

## Step 5 — Enable MFA If Not Already Active

If the account in question does not have multi-factor authentication (MFA) active, enable it now. Even if your password was captured, MFA prevents the attacker from logging in without the second factor.

## Step 6 — Watch for Follow-On Indicators

In the hours and days after clicking a suspicious link, watch for:
- Emails sent from your account that you did not send.
- Password reset notifications for accounts you did not request.
- Unexpected charges or payment activity.
- Colleagues receiving unusual emails that appear to come from you.

Report any of these to your supervisor immediately.

## The Short Version

Click → Disconnect if download → Report immediately → Change password → Enable MFA → Watch for follow-on signs.

The worst response is to do nothing and hope it was harmless. The best response is fast reporting.
