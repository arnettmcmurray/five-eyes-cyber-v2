# Response Card: I Entered My Credentials on a Suspicious Page

## Your account may already be compromised. Every minute counts.

---

**1. Report to your supervisor or security contact immediately.**
Do not handle this alone. Tell them: which account, what page you entered credentials on, approximate time.

**2. Change the password on the affected account right now.**
Use a different device if possible (in case your current device is compromised).
- New password: use your password manager, or pick a long passphrase (4+ random words).
- If you used the same password on other accounts, change those too.

**3. Revoke active sessions.**
Most services have a "Sign out of all devices" or "Active sessions" option in account settings. Use it. This invalidates any session the attacker may have established.

**4. Review account settings for changes.**
- Email: check for forwarding rules, filters, and rules that auto-forward to external addresses.
- Load board or TMS: check for new contacts, changed settings, or unauthorized load activity.
- Any finance portal: check for bank detail changes, new payees, or pending transactions.

**5. Enable or reset MFA on the affected account.**
If MFA was not active, enable it immediately. If it was active, reset your MFA devices — the attacker may have been able to add their own recovery device.

**6. Notify contacts if appropriate.**
If your email account was compromised, your customers and vendors may receive emails from your account asking them to take action. Alert your key contacts that your account may have been used fraudulently and tell them to disregard any unusual emails from you.

**7. Document what happened.**
Write down: the email or text you received, what page you entered credentials on, the time, what actions the page prompted, and any changes you noticed in your account after. This documentation helps your IT/MSP and, if needed, law enforcement.

---

This is a serious incident. Treat it as one. Fast, complete response is the right approach.
