# How to Report a Suspicious Email, Text, or Call

Reporting suspicious communications is one of the highest-value security actions anyone in the company can take. A single report can stop an active attack before it reaches more targets. This policy defines what to report, how to report it, and what to expect after you report.

## What to Report

Report any of the following:

- An email asking you to log in via a link and you are not certain the link is legitimate.
- An email requesting a payment, bank detail change, or wire transfer — especially if it is unexpected or creates urgency.
- A text or call asking you to provide login credentials, a one-time verification code, or personal/banking information.
- Any communication claiming your account, authority, or compliance is at risk and demanding immediate action.
- An attachment you did not expect — especially from an unknown sender or from a known sender acting unusually.
- A call or text asking for your location, load details, or delivery instructions from an unverified contact.
- Any communication you acted on that may have been fraudulent (you clicked a link, entered credentials, or sent information). **This is especially important to report immediately.**

## How to Report

1. **Do not forward the suspicious email to coworkers** — this spreads the risk.
2. **Do not click or interact further** with the suspicious communication.
3. **Preserve the evidence** — do not delete the email or text.
4. **Report to your supervisor or designated security contact** with the following details:
   - What you received (email, text, call)
   - Who it appeared to be from (sender name and actual address/number)
   - What it asked you to do
   - Whether you took any action (clicked, entered information, responded)
   - The time and date

For email: if your email client supports "Report phishing" or "Report spam," use that function and then notify your supervisor separately.

## If You Already Clicked or Responded

Tell your supervisor or security contact immediately — do not wait. If you entered credentials, a one-time code, or banking information, every minute matters. Fast reporting enables fast account recovery. There is no blame for reporting — there is only risk from not reporting.

See the "I clicked a link" and "I entered my credentials" response cards in the KB for immediate next steps.

## What Happens After You Report

Your supervisor or security contact will:
- Assess whether an incident response needs to be opened.
- Alert other team members if the same attack may be targeting them.
- Work with IT or your MSP to investigate if accounts were affected.
- File reports with relevant parties (bank, law enforcement) if financial fraud is involved.

Reports that turn out to be harmless are never a problem. Reports that stop active attacks are exactly what makes this training worthwhile.

## Culture Note

Reporting is not about blame. Someone who clicks a link and immediately reports it has done the right thing. Someone who clicks, gets compromised, and does not report because they are embarrassed creates a much worse outcome for the company.

Good security is a team practice. Report what you see.
