# Response Card: My Account May Be Compromised

## Signs: unexpected login alerts, emails you did not send, MFA prompts you did not request, contacts reporting strange messages from your account.

---

**1. Report to your supervisor immediately.**

**2. Change the password on the affected account.**
Do this from a different device if possible.
Use a new, unique password — not a variation of the old one.

**3. Revoke all active sessions.**
Look for "Sign out of all devices" or "Active sessions" in account security settings.
This logs out any session the attacker may have open.

**4. Check for changes made by the attacker:**
- Email: look for forwarding rules, filters, new auto-replies, inbox rules
- TMS/load board: look for changed settings, new users added, modified load data
- Payment portal: look for new payees, pending transactions, bank detail changes

**5. Reset MFA.**
Remove all MFA devices associated with the account.
Re-add your own device fresh.

**6. Notify contacts if relevant.**
If your email was used to send fraudulent messages to customers, vendors, or carriers — contact them and tell them to disregard any unusual messages from your account.

**7. Enable MFA if it was not active.**
This is required before the account goes back into service.

---

Do not put the account back in service until steps 2–6 are complete.
