# Internal Escalation Chain — Who to Call and When

When something looks wrong — a payment diverted, a system behaving unexpectedly, a carrier record modified without explanation — your first instinct matters. The escalation chain below tells you exactly who to contact and how fast.

## The Escalation Chain

**Any employee → Immediate supervisor → IT/security contact → Operations leadership → Legal**

Every step must happen in order. Do not skip directly to leadership without involving IT. Do not involve legal before leadership has been notified.

## Time Triggers

The speed of your escalation must match the type of incident:

**Financial fraud (BEC, wire redirect, unauthorized payment instruction, cargo theft):**
Escalate to your supervisor and IT/security within **30 minutes** of discovery. Wire fraud has a recovery window measured in hours, not days. Speed is the difference between recovering the funds and losing them permanently.

**System compromise (ransomware, credential theft, unauthorized account access, unknown admin activity):**
Contact IT/security within **15 minutes**. An active attacker inside your systems compounds damage with every minute of access. Do not continue working in the affected system while escalating.

## What to Capture While Escalating

Write this down on paper if needed — do not rely on memory:

- The time you first noticed the issue (as precise as possible)
- What you saw — exactly what appeared on screen, or what the email said, or what the system showed
- Which device you were on (laptop, phone, shared terminal)
- Which system or application was involved (TMS, email, load board, ELD portal)
- What actions you took or did not take before escalating

This information is evidence. IT and law enforcement will need it.

## Who Does Not Make External Reporting Decisions

Front-line employees and supervisors do not decide whether to report an incident to the FBI, regulators, customers, or the press. That decision belongs to operations leadership and legal counsel, who must assess regulatory obligations, scope, and timing.

Your job is to escalate accurately and fast. Their job is to determine what happens next outside the organization.

## Evidence Handling During Escalation

- Do not delete, move, or modify any files, emails, or records related to the incident
- Do not run antivirus scans or reboot affected systems on your own
- Do not forward suspicious emails to colleagues for their opinion — forward only to IT

## The One Thing That Creates Legal Exposure

If you discover an incident and tell a colleague to keep quiet about it while you "figure it out," that is not incident response — it is suppression. If the incident later causes customer harm or regulatory exposure, that conversation becomes a liability. Escalate. Every time.
