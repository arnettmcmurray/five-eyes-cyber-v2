# Exception Handling Rules: Destination Changes, Carrier Swaps, and Reconsignment

In normal freight operations, exceptions happen: customers change delivery requirements, carriers have breakdowns, facilities have schedule changes. Fraudsters know this — and use these legitimate exception patterns as the cover for their attacks. This policy defines the verification requirements for high-risk exceptions.

## Why Exceptions Are a High-Risk Attack Surface

NMFTA operational guidance identifies late-stage changes as high-risk and calls for layered, repeatable controls and traceable workflows. The reason: exceptions bypass the normal process controls established at load booking. A fraudulent exception that is accepted at face value can redirect a load, change a payment destination, or hand cargo to an unauthorized party.

The rule: the more unusual an exception, the more verification it requires — not less.

## Controlled Exceptions: Required Verification for Each

### Destination Change or Reconsignment

A request to change the delivery destination after a load is in transit.

**Required verification:**
1. The request must come from the original booking contact — the person or organization that arranged the load — not from a new contact who claims authority.
2. Verify by calling the original booking contact at their established number. Do not verify by replying to the email requesting the change.
3. Two-person approval for reconsignment: your supervisor or operations manager must approve the change.
4. Document: who authorized the change, when, and through what verification method.

**Red flags:** Reconsignment requests that arrive by text or a new email thread, that are framed as urgent, or that come from a contact you do not have a prior relationship with.

### Carrier Swap or Driver Change After Confirmation

A request to substitute a different driver or carrier for one that was already confirmed.

**Required verification:**
1. Call your carrier contact at the verified number in your dispatch record.
2. Confirm they authorized the substitution and that the new driver/equipment is their legitimate employee or leased driver.
3. Obtain the new driver's name and equipment identifiers before they arrive.
4. Update the pickup code (if your process allows) or confirm the existing code was shared with the new driver through the carrier — not through the requester.

**Red flags:** A swap request that arrives from a contact you did not confirm as the carrier's authorized representative, or that includes a request to use a "different contact" than the one in your file.

### New Banking or Payment Instructions for an In-Process Load

A request to change payment routing after a load is accepted but before settlement.

**Required verification:** Same as BEC payment policy — independent callback, dual approval if required. No payment changes based solely on an email instruction.

**Red flags:** Payment change requests described as urgent, associated with a new entity name, or accompanied by pressure not to follow normal verification.

### Release to an Alternate Consignee

A request to release the load to a different entity than the consignee on the BOL.

**Required verification:**
1. Written authorization from the original shipper or freight owner, obtained through a verified channel (not just an email thread).
2. Supervisor approval.
3. Document the alternate consignee: name, ID presented, contact information.

**Red flags:** Any request to release to an alternate consignee that arrives by text or that cannot be traced back to the original shipper through a verified contact.

## General Exception Rules

1. **Every exception involving a change to destination, carrier, payment, or consignee requires verification at least one level up** from the normal approval chain — i.e., if a dispatcher normally handles routine loads, an exception goes to the operations manager.

2. **No exception is authorized based solely on an email or text** that you cannot verify through an established, independent contact.

3. **Urgency is not a valid reason to skip verification.** If an exception is genuinely urgent, it can withstand a 10-minute verification call.

4. **Document every exception** — who requested it, what was changed, how it was verified, who approved it.

5. **When in doubt, hold.** A delayed load is recoverable. A misdirected load may not be.
