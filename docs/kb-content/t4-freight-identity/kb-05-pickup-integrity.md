# Pickup Integrity: Matching Driver, Equipment, and Seal Before Release

The pickup moment is the last control point before cargo leaves your custody. NMFTA operational guidance emphasizes that verifying driver identity, tractor and trailer identifiers, and seal numbers — and treating late-stage changes as high-risk — is foundational to preventing cargo theft-by-deception. This article defines what must be verified before any load is released.

## The Five Pickup Integrity Checks

### Check 1: Driver Identity

**What to verify:** The driver's name on their CDL or government-issued ID matches the driver name on the load appointment record in your TMS.

**How:** Ask for CDL. Compare name to load record. Do not accept a "the driver changed last minute" explanation without re-verification (see the exception handling policy).

**Why:** A fraudulent driver carrying convincing paperwork but a different identity than what was scheduled is a major warning sign. Fictitious pickup operations rely on the dock not checking whether the arriving driver matches the scheduled one.

### Check 2: Tractor Plate or VIN

**What to verify:** The tractor's license plate (or VIN for some loads) matches what the carrier confirmed with dispatch in advance.

**How:** Confirm the tractor plate with dispatch at the time the carrier is confirmed — not at the time of pickup. At pickup, the dock personnel compare the arriving plate against the record.

**Why:** A fraudulent carrier may arrive with a different tractor than the one the legitimate carrier uses. If the plate does not match — stop and call dispatch before releasing.

### Check 3: Trailer Plate or Number

**What to verify:** The trailer number or plate matches what is in the load record.

**How:** Same as tractor — confirm in advance, verify at pickup. For drop-trailers, the trailer number is confirmed when the load is tendered to the trailer.

### Check 4: Seal Number

**What to verify:** For sealed trailers, the seal number is recorded on the BOL before the trailer leaves. A seal that does not match at the delivery point is evidence of potential tampering.

**How:** Dock staff records the seal number on the BOL at time of sealing. This becomes part of the document of record for the load.

**Why:** Seal number tracking establishes a chain of custody record. If a load is claimed to have been tampered with, the seal number provides evidence of when and where access occurred.

### Check 5: Pickup Code

**What to verify:** The driver provides the correct pickup code that was communicated to the carrier in advance through a separate channel (not in the same email or text chain as the load details).

**How:** Generate a simple, unique code for each pickup and communicate it directly to the carrier's verified dispatch contact. The dock releases only to drivers who know this code.

**Why:** Pickup codes are the single most effective control against fictitious pickups. A fraudulent driver who has obtained the load details (through a compromised email, a double-brokering chain, or a stolen load board account) will know the load details — but will not know a code that was communicated through a separate verified channel.

## When Equipment or Driver Does Not Match

Do not release the load if any of the following are true:
- The arriving driver's name does not match the load appointment.
- The tractor or trailer identifiers do not match.
- The driver does not know the pickup code.

Steps:
1. Politely inform the driver that you need to verify through dispatch.
2. Call your dispatch contact at the number in your records.
3. Do not allow the driver to leave with the load until verification is complete.
4. Document what the arriving driver presented (name, CDL number, tractor plate, trailer plate, any paperwork they have).
5. If the verification call reveals a discrepancy — escalate to your supervisor and do not release.

## Last-Minute Change Requests

A last-minute message (especially via text or a new email thread) saying "the driver changed — new driver is [name] in [tractor]" requires re-verification:
- Call your dispatch contact on the established number.
- Confirm the change is legitimate.
- Get the new driver's information on record before they arrive.

A last-minute change that cannot be verified through your established dispatch contact is a strong indicator of a fraud attempt, not a routine operational adjustment.
