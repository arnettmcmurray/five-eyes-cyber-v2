# Double Brokering: How It Works and How to Detect It

Double brokering is a fraudulent practice where a broker or carrier who has accepted a load brokers it to a second carrier without the shipper's knowledge or consent. The result: the shipper loses visibility and accountability for their freight, carriers may go unpaid, and loads may end up with carriers who were never vetted. FMCSA treats unauthorized double brokering as a violation — operating as an unregistered broker and potentially as identity theft.

## How Legitimate Brokering Works

In normal operations:
- A shipper contracts with a licensed broker (FMCSA-registered, bonded) to move a load.
- The broker arranges a carrier and provides load details.
- The broker takes responsibility for the carrier relationship.
- Settlement flows: shipper pays broker, broker pays carrier.

## How Double Brokering Works

1. **The fraudulent intermediary** accepts a load from a shipper or broker claiming to be a carrier.
2. **They re-post the load** on a load board, acting as a broker — a function they are not licensed or bonded to perform.
3. **A second carrier** accepts the load from the intermediary, not knowing they are dealing with an unregistered broker rather than the shipper or authorized broker.
4. **The second carrier hauls the load** and delivers it.
5. **The intermediary disappears** — either without paying the second carrier, without remitting to the original shipper/broker, or both.

The shipper may get their freight delivered but find themselves in a payment dispute. The second carrier delivered the load but was never properly contracted by the party they thought they were working for. The intermediary may have collected payment from the shipper/broker while skipping payment to the carrier.

## The Identity Theft Variant

A more dangerous variant involves an attacker creating a fraudulent carrier profile using a stolen USDOT/MC number from a legitimate carrier. They:
- Use the legitimate carrier's credentials to accept loads on load boards.
- Operate either as double-brokerers (re-brokering to unknown second carriers) or as direct theft operations (accepting loads and not delivering them).

The legitimate carrier does not know their identity is being used until they receive complaints from shippers or are contacted about loads they never accepted.

FMCSA explicitly treats this as identity theft and misuse of authority — a legal violation beyond the financial fraud.

## How to Detect It

**At the tender stage:**
- Verify the carrier's FMCSA registration before tendering. Confirm their authority is active and for property transport.
- Note the specific MC number and DOT number you verified. Any discrepancy at pickup means the arriving carrier is not who you contracted with.

**At pickup:**
- The driver must present equipment that matches what was confirmed with your contracted carrier.
- If the driver presents a rate confirmation or carrier authority showing a different company name or MC number than what you have on file — stop. Do not release the load.
- Contact your contracted carrier directly at their verified number before proceeding.

**In settlement:**
- If payment disputes arise and you discover multiple parties claim to have brokered the same load — this is a double brokering indicator. Document everything and contact your legal/compliance resource.

**Pattern indicators:**
- Carrier cannot describe the load's origin or details accurately.
- Carrier refers you to "their dispatcher" at a number that does not match your file.
- Pickup paperwork shows different company name or contact information than what you arranged.

## What to Do If You Suspect Double Brokering

1. Do not release the load to the arriving carrier until you have spoken directly with your contracted carrier or broker.
2. Document what the arriving driver presents (name, CDL number, tractor plate, trailer plate, the paperwork they have).
3. Contact your verified dispatch or broker contact on the number in your records.
4. If you cannot verify the arriving carrier's legitimacy — hold the load and escalate to your supervisor.

A delayed pickup is a small problem. A stolen load is a large one.
