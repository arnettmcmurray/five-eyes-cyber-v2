# Your Vendor Was Breached — Do This Within 24 Hours

A vendor breach notification means an attacker may have had access to credentials, data, or communication channels that reach your systems. Act now. Do not wait for the vendor to tell you exactly what was exposed — that information may take days or weeks to arrive, and your window for early containment is measured in hours.

---

**1. Identify what access that vendor had**

Open your vendor access register immediately. Find the vendor. Note every system they could access, their access level, and the named contact. If the register is not current, pull the systems you know they touch and work from that list.

---

**2. Rotate all shared credentials that vendor could have accessed**

Change any shared passwords, API keys, or tokens that vendor held access to — regardless of whether you believe they were used. If the vendor's systems were compromised, any credential they stored is potentially exposed. Rotate your side now. This is the one control you fully own.

---

**3. Review access logs for vendor activity in the last 30 days**

Request access logs from your MSP, cloud provider, or TMS for the vendor's account(s). Look for: logins at unusual hours, bulk data exports, permission changes, or access from IP addresses inconsistent with the vendor's known location. You are looking for evidence of unauthorized use of the vendor's credentials against your systems.

---

**4. Notify your cyber insurer**

A vendor breach that reaches your systems or data is a covered event trigger under most cyber policies. Call your insurer or your broker today — do not wait until you have full details. Early notification preserves coverage and starts the clock on insurer-provided incident response support if needed.

---

**5. Alert your team: watch for spoofed vendor emails and payment change requests**

Brief your dispatch, AP, and operations staff immediately. Tell them: a vendor you work with has been breached; any email claiming to be from that vendor should be verified by phone before any action is taken; payment change requests from that vendor are frozen pending manual verification.

---

**6. Update your vendor access register**

Log the breach notification date, what systems were potentially affected, and the remediation actions you have taken. This creates the audit trail your insurer, any regulatory inquiry, and your own future review will need.

---

**7. Request a written post-incident report from the vendor**

Once the vendor has completed their investigation, request a written report describing: what systems were compromised, what data may have been exposed, what timeframe the attacker had access, and what remediation the vendor has implemented. This document belongs in your vendor file. If the vendor cannot or will not provide it, that informs how much trust you extend to this relationship going forward.

---

**If you cannot confirm within 24 hours that vendor access to your systems is fully contained, treat this as an active incident and contact your cyber insurer for incident response support.**
