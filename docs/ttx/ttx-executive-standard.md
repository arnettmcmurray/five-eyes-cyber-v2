# Five Eyes TTX Executive Standard

The Five Eyes Tabletop Exercise (TTX) system is not a sandbox; it is a **Business Resilience Management System**. This document defines the standard for "executive-grade" TTX content and execution within the platform.

## 1. Instructional Design Philosophy
- **Decision-Centric**: Every scenario must pivot on high-stakes leadership trade-offs (e.g., "Operations vs. Containment").
- **Physical-Cyber Convergence**: Scenarios should reflect the reality of modern threats where physical access and digital compromise are indistinguishable.
- **Role-Based Objectives**: Objectives must be mapped to specific organizational functions (Legal, Ops, IT, Executive).

## 2. Scenario Architecture (The "Opsbook" Model)
All flagship scenarios must follow the hierarchical structure:
1.  **Narrative Arc**: A multi-phase story (intro -> discovery -> escalation -> resolution).
2.  **Sections**: Logical time-blocks or phases of the incident.
3.  **Steps**: The atomic unit of facilitation. Each step contains:
    - Facilitator Narrative (Read aloud).
    - Participant Prompts (Guided discussion).
    - Technical/Strategic Background.
4.  **Injects**: Discrete "shocks" to the system (Media leaks, legal notices, additional telemetry) delivered at planned intervals.

## 3. Consequence Logic
- **Branching Decisions**: While the narrative is guided, certain "Decision Points" should have documented consequences that alter the AAR outcomes.
- **Trade-off Analysis**: Facilitators should be prompted to challenge decisions (e.g., "If you disconnect the vendor, you lose 40% of production capacity. Proceed?").

## 4. After-Action Reporting (AAR) Standard
The product of a TTX is the **Action Catalog**, not a PDF. AARs must:
- Extract **Objective-by-Objective Analysis**.
- Identify **Gaps in Playbooks**.
- Produce **Tracked Remediation Items** with owners and deadlines.
- Provide **Board-Ready Executive Summaries**.

## 5. Knowledge Base (KB) Grounding
- All scenario prompts and injects should ideally be "grounded" in the platform's Knowledge Base.
- Participants should be encouraged to search the KB for relevant policies (e.g., "What is our 48-hour notification policy?") during the exercise.
